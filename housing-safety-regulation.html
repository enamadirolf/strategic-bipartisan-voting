When Housing Supply, Public Order, and Regulation Pull Apart

Many people hold several of these views at once. The offices that control them are not the same.

Common overlapping priorities

  • More housing supply and fewer local barriers to building, especially in high-cost areas.
  • Criminal-justice changes that reduce long sentences for non-violent offenses while still wanting effective responses to organized theft, open-air drug markets, and violent crime.
  • Environmental or labor standards that do not produce large increases in energy costs or sharp reductions in housing and infrastructure construction.
  • Support for legal immigration pathways alongside concern about wage pressure or strained local services in specific sectors and places.

These positions frequently conflict across party lines and across levels of government. A straight-ticket vote requires sacrificing at least one of them.

Which offices actually matter

Housing supply is controlled mainly by city councils, zoning boards, and state legislatures (preemption of local barriers). Federal policy is secondary.

Local public safety and prosecution priorities are set by district attorneys, sheriffs, mayors, and city councils. State legislatures can change sentencing ranges and pretrial rules.

Environmental, energy, and labor rules that affect costs are shaped by federal agencies and Congress, state legislatures and governors, and sometimes local ordinances.

Immigration enforcement and legal pathways are primarily federal (Congress and the executive), with state and local cooperation decisions affecting day-to-day intensity.

Because the levers sit in different places, it is possible to advance more than one priority by choosing different candidates in different races.

Applying the method

  1. Rank the priorities above (or your own version of them) in order.
  2. For each high-ranked item, note the one or two offices with the most direct control.
  3. In those specific races, compare the candidates on the relevant power rather than on party label.
  4. Accept a split ticket if that is what the mapping produces.

The same process works if your ordering is different. The point is to match votes to offices instead of matching votes to a single party.
